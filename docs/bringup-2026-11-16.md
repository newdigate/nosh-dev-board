# Nosh bring-up, 2026-11-16

First power-up of the assembled Nosh board (JLCPCB order Y95-2611336A, 5 PCB + 2 PCBA,
placed 2026-11-03). One board on the bench, powered from the USB-C device port J3, Atmel-ICE
on the SWD header J1. No console adapter on J11 and no microSD card were available, so the
verdicts below were read out of RAM over SWD rather than from the serial transcript.

## Result in one line

The board boots `nosh_bringup` from its QSPI NOR with SW4 = 10, the user LED blinks at 2 Hz,
the 128 MB SDRAM passes its data, address and fill tests, the microSD mounts and round-trips a
file, and nothing on the board needed a rework. By the end of the day the sketch reports
`BRINGUP=PASS`. The one defect found was in software: a NACK race in the Wire library's
LPI2C driver that made a bus scan report every second address, fixed and verified the same
day (Wire 0d80418).

## 1. The charge-only USB-C cable

Symptom: the board was clearly powered (chip answering over SWD, see below) but the Mac never
enumerated anything from J3, in any boot mode.

Cause: the first USB-C cable carried VBUS but no D+/D-. Swapping to a known-good data cable
made the ROM serial downloader appear immediately as `SE Blank RT Family`, VID 0x1fc9
PID 0x013d.

Lesson: a Nosh that powers up but shows no USB device is first of all a cable question. The
board side of J3 (16-pin USB 2.0 receptacle, Rd 5.1 k on both CC pins, RClamp0854P ESD) is
fine.

## 2. Boot-mode switch SW4

SW4 is the KingTek DSHP02TS 2-way slide DIP. Pad-to-net mapping from the PCB:

| SW4 position | Pads | Series resistor | Strap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 and 4 | R11 4.7 k | BOOT_MODE1 (GPIO_LPSR_03) |
| 2 | 2 and 3 | R12 4.7 k | BOOT_MODE0 (GPIO_LPSR_02) |

Pads 1 and 2 are +3V3; both straps have 100 k pull-downs, so "off" reads 0.

| BOOT_MODE[1:0] | SW4 | ROM behaviour on Nosh |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | both off | "boot from fuses". What was observed on the day: both cores parked in branch-to-self loops (CM7 at 0x00223104, CM4 at 0x0023f040), SRC_SCR with BT_RELEASE_M4 set, nothing on USB. **That was pyOCD, not the ROM** (see section 6d): its RT1176 target parks both cores on exactly those two addresses and releases the CM4 on every connect, and the USB silence was the charge-only cable. **Re-tested 2026-11-17 with the probe off J1 and a data cable in J3: 00 enumerates the serial downloader (`SE Blank RT Family` 1fc9:013d), exactly as the RM says for blank fuses.** |
| 01 | pos 2 on | serial downloader on USB1/J3, enumerates as 1fc9:013d |
| 10 | pos 1 on | internal boot from the W25Q512 on FlexSPI1 |

The boot-mode word is `SRC_SBMR2` at 0x40C0400C, BMOD in bits 25:24. Reading it is the
quick way to confirm what the ROM latched after a reset.

## 3. Flashing over SWD with pyOCD

pyOCD's built-in RT1170 target has a 16 MB FlexSPI flash region at 0x30000000 (4 KB sectors,
256 B pages) and its loader programmed the 3.3 V W25Q512JV without complaint.

Do **not** hand pyOCD the ELF. The core's ITCM code (`.text.itcm`) has VMA 0 and LMA
0x300022d0; a section-by-VMA loader would skip it or try to write ITCM. Build a flash-only
binary by load address first:

```bash
arm-none-eabi-objcopy -O binary \
  -j .text.headers -j .text.code -j .text.itcm -j .fini -j .ARM.exidx -j .data -j .text.csf \
  build-nosh/nosh_bringup.elf nosh_bringup.bin
```

`.vectorsram`, `.bss`, `.bss.dma` and `.bss.extram` are RAM-only and must stay out. Check the
image before programming: the FCB tag `FCFB` at offset 0x400 and the IVT tag 0xD1 at 0x1000
are what the RT1170 ROM looks for.

```bash
pyocd flash -t mimxrt1170_cm7 -a 0x30000000 -e sector nosh_bringup.bin
```

Result for the 55,296-byte image: 14 sectors erased, 216 pages programmed, about 7.8 kB/s.
Read-back with `pyocd cmd -t mimxrt1170_cm7 -M attach -c "savemem 0x30000000 55296 rb.bin"`
compared byte-identical.

The serial downloader path (SW4 = 01, SPSDK `blhost` plus the RT1170 flashloader) is the
documented alternative in the README; it was not exercised because SPSDK is not installed
here and the SWD path worked first time.

## 6. Open items

**microSD on uSDHC2.** Done, see the passing run above.

**I2C5 scan: every odd address acknowledges. FIXED the same day, verified on the board:
`I2C5_SCAN=DONE n=0` with the rebuilt image (build Nov 16 2026 13:41:23).** It was a
driver defect, not the bus. In
`Wire/lpi2c1176.c`, `lpi2c1176_master_write()` judges ACK/NACK by waiting for the STOP
(SDF) with NDF as the error mask. When the LPI2C master sees a NACK it sets NDF and
generates a STOP on its own; `lpi2c1176_wait_flag()` clears NDF and returns the moment it
sees it, and `lpi2c1176_bus_recover()` resets the FIFOs, both before that hardware STOP has
completed. The next transaction (the odd address) queues START and STOP, waits for SDF, and
is satisfied by the previous transaction's STOP completing, so it returns 0 = ACK. Its own
NACK is then seen correctly by the transaction after it, and the pattern alternates: n = 56
of 112, all odd. The fix (Wire commit 0d80418, pinned in `evkb.cmake`): on NDF,
`wait_flag` waits for SDF before clearing anything; `bus_recover` waits for MBF to clear before
resetting the FIFOs; `master_read` no longer queues a second STOP after an address NACK. The
QEMU gate still passes (its scan finds the three modelled devices) and the board reports
n = 0, which is the right answer for a bus with only an unclocked SGTL5000 on it.

**Two reflash lessons from the second image.**

- *Readback after a flash is skewed.* The pyOCD flash algorithm leaves FlexSPI in its own
  configuration, and AHB reads from 0x30000000 then come back shifted by one byte from the
  FCB onwards (every sampled 256-byte block matched the image at shift +1). The content was
  fine. A readback compare is only meaningful while an image booted by the ROM is running,
  as it was after the first flash. pyOCD's own "pages skipped" count is unreliable for the
  same reason.
- *A freshly flashed image does not boot until SW1.* After pyOCD's post-flash reset the ROM
  wrote AIRCR SYSRESETREQ (0x05FA0004) and spun at 0x002230e8 waiting for a reset that never
  came, because SRC_SRMR held M7REQ_RESET_MODE = "do not reset". The first reading of this
  blamed the ROM and a skewed header read; the real actor is pyOCD, see section 6d. Only SW1
  or a power cycle boots a freshly flashed image; budget for a button press after every flash.

## 6d. What pyOCD's RT1176 target does on every connect

Found while trying to make host-side resets work. pyOCD's built-in `mimxrt1170_cm7` target
(`target_MIMXRT1176xxxxx.py`, `find_aps`) performs three writes each time it connects:

1. `prepare_cm7_trap_code`: a two-word vector table at 0x2001FF00 whose reset entry is
   0x223105, and `IOMUXC_LPSR_GPR26` (the CM7 boot VTOR) pointed at it. 0x223104 is a
   `b .` inside the ROM, which is why the CM7 "parked" there.
2. `release_cm4` + `prepare_cm4_trap_code`: SRC_SCR.BT_RELEASE_M4 = 1 with a vector table at
   0x20250000 whose reset entry is 0x23F041, another ROM `b .`. That is the "CM4 running at
   0x0023f040" and the BT_RELEASE_M4 bit read earlier.
3. `update_sbmr`: ORs 0xF << 10 into SRC_SRMR (it calls it SBMR), i.e. M4REQ and M7REQ reset
   mode = "do not reset anything". That is the 0x3c00 and why every SYSRESETREQ, from pyOCD,
   from the ROM or from firmware, was swallowed while a probe session had touched the chip.

Consequences and corrections:

- The "SW4 = 00 parks both cores" observation in section 2 was this trap code, taken through a
  pyOCD session. It says nothing about the ROM's boot-from-fuses behaviour.
- A power-on reset from SW1 clears GPR26 and SRMR, which is why the button always worked.
- Clearing the mask from firmware was tried and reverted: pyOCD re-arms it on the next attach,
  and with the mask cleared and GPR26 zeroed in the same session, a SYSRESETREQ did reset the
  SoC (SRSR records M7_REQUEST) but the ROM came up in its own park loop at 0x223104 with the
  FCB and IVT perfectly readable through FlexSPI. (Zeroing GPR26 was itself wrong: its reset
  value is 0x4000, the ROM at 0x00200000. Section 6r repeats the test cleanly; the ROM still
  parks, and a host-side start from the park is now the working reset.)
- A user script that no-ops the three hooks (`-O user_script=pyocd_notrap.py`, in the session
  scratch) keeps SRMR clean across attaches but does not change the ROM's warm-reset
  behaviour, so it does not buy a host-side reboot either.
- Net (superseded by section 6r): on this bench, flash with pyOCD, then press SW1. Reads over
  SWD remain safe and useful; just remember that any attach rewrites GPR26 and SRMR behind your
  back.

**SGTL5000 did not answer at 0x0A.** Expected here. The sketch never starts SAI1, so the
codec has no SYS_MCLK, and the SGTL5000's control port needs MCLK running; the Teensy Audio
library always has I2S up before `enable()`. The audio examples (`tone_test`,
`i2s_audio_test`) are the real test of the codec and of the LPI2C5 wiring to it.

## 6a. tone_test: the PIT clock was 20x off, on the EVKB too

`audio/tone_test` is not an audio test: it drives a square wave on Arduino pin 9 (GPIO_AD_01,
the unpopulated touch panel's reset line on Nosh) with `tone()` and reads the pad back
through the GPIO data register. First run on the board:

```
e1=3975 e2=7999 e3=0 during=200 after=0
TONE=FAIL
```

1 kHz requested, 20 kHz measured; 2 kHz requested, 40 kHz measured; a 100 ms timed tone that
stopped after 5 ms. Everything was a factor 20 fast. `tone()` sits on `IntervalTimer`, which
sits on PIT1, which is clocked by the BUS clock root. Live registers: `CCM_CLOCK_ROOT2` =
0x401 (mux 4 = SYS_PLL3 480 MHz, divide by 2 = 240 MHz, exactly what `startup.c` programs)
and PIT1 `LDVAL0` = 5999 for a 500 µs half period, i.e. the firmware believed the PIT ran at
12 MHz. The decoder in `IntervalTimer.cpp` (`pit_clock_hz`) only knew mux 0 and treated every
other source as 24 MHz. QEMU's `imxrt_ccm.c` had the identical gap, so the model ran PIT1 at
12 MHz too and the QEMU gate agreed with the wrong firmware. The hardware was the first thing
to disagree.

This is an EVKB bug as much as a Nosh one: every `IntervalTimer` period and every `tone()`
on the RT1176 core has been 20x short on silicon. The QEMU gates could never have caught it
because the model mirrored the firmware's assumption.

Fix (uncommitted at the time of writing): `pit_clock_hz` decodes all eight BUS sources per the
RM (fsl_clock.h `kCLOCK_BUS_ClockRoot_Mux*`, PFD3 computed from the ANADIG FRAC field) and
`imxrt_ccm.c` gets the same table so the model matches silicon. Rebuilt and reflashed:

```
e1=198 e2=399 e3=0 during=99 after=0
TONE=PASS
```

PIT1 `LDVAL0` now reads 0x1D4BF = 119,999 ticks for 500 µs, which is 240 MHz. The counts are
the same numbers the QEMU gate has always printed, now for the right reason.

## 6h. Bluetooth HCI on the M.2 card over LPUART2 (2026-11-17)

`examples/networking/m2_hci_probe` built with `-DEVKB_BOARD=nosh`, both IW416 blobs
(`-DM2RADIO_IW416_FW=.../sdIW416_wlan.bin.inc -DM2RADIO_IW416_BT_FW=.../uartIW416_bt.bin.inc`,
445 KB image), flashed over SWD, SW1.

First run, default options: `bt_fw_download=ok` (142 chunks, 131840 bytes, 0 CRC errors, the
card's own chunk requests received back, so LPUART2 is bidirectional through U11/U12), Wi-Fi
side up as before, then `hci_reset=timeout reason=no_response` at 3 M / 921600 / 460800 /
115200 baud with framing=0 throughout. Live registers over SWD: LPUART2 MODIR = 0 (RXRTSE off),
GPIO_DISP_B2_13 muxed ALT3 = LPUART2_RTS_B. With hardware RTS off that pad idles HIGH, and on
Nosh it reaches the card's CTS input through U11. The IW416 bootloader ignores CTS; the booted
firmware does not transmit while CTS is high. The probe's own `M2_BT_ASSERT_CTS` option
documents exactly this.

Second run, `-DM2_BT_ASSERT_CTS=ON`:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| HCI Reset | `hci_reset=ok attempts=1 timeouts=0 framing=0` |
| Read_Local_Version | hci_ver 15, hci_rev 0x8300, lmp_ver 15, manufacturer 0x0048, lmp_subver 0x109B |
| Read_BD_ADDR | A0:CD:F3:C5:B9:F3 (Wi-Fi MAC + 1) |
| Read_Buffer_Size | ACL 1021 x 7, SCO 120 x 6 |
| Inquiry (GIAC 10.24 s) | complete, 0 devices (nothing discoverable nearby) |
| Heartbeat | `hci=ok`, all counters 0 |

Nosh note: on the EVKB this pad also resets the 1 Gb Ethernet PHY, which is why the assert was
an opt-in GPIO hack there. On Nosh BT_UART_RTS is dedicated and level-shifted to the module, so

**INT edge excess: explained and fixed (2026-11-18).** Every hardware run, on both boards,
counted a few more rising INT edges than buffers read (7 to 29), so `TOUCH_OK` was never printed.

1. *Not a floating line.* The RT1176 core's `pinMode(INPUT_PULLUP)` does not enable a pull:
   it writes pad value 0x0008 for every mode, which on a GPIO_AD pad is PUS=1 with PUE=0
   (pull disabled). Writing 0x0C (PUE + PUS, weak pull-up) to `SW_PAD_CTL_PAD_GPIO_AD_00`
   directly, read back as `INT_PAD=0x0000000C`, left the excess unchanged (241 / 230). The
   GT911 drives the line.
2. *Timestamped edges.* A scratch build logged every edge (DWT cycle count) and every poll
   result, poll gap and phase boundary, read back over SWD. The GT911 pulses INT once per scan,
   every 10.2 ms (9.4 to 11.3 ms). 208 of 209 buffers paired one-to-one with an edge; the 209th
   was the last read, whose edge came after the count. All 10 unpaired edges fell in the six
   29-39 ms gaps where the test stops polling to repaint (after each of the five targets, once
   during the swipe):

   | Gap | Edges in gap | Unpaired |
   |---|---|---|
   | 29 ms, after target 1 | 2 | 1 |
   | 35 ms, after target 2 | 4 | 2 |
   | 39 ms, after target 3 | 3 | 2 |
   | 32 ms, after target 4 | 3 | 2 |
   | 29 ms, after target 5 | 3 | 1 |
   | 35 ms, during the swipe | 3 | 2 |

   The GT911 keeps pulsing every scan while its single buffer is unacknowledged, so a pause of
   about three scans gives three edges and one buffer. The QEMU model pulses once per
   acknowledged buffer, which is why the gate's `BUFFERS <= INT_EDGES <= BUFFERS + POLL_FAILS + 1`
   holds in QEMU and never on glass.
3. *Fix in the test, evkb `9529295`.* The ISR counts an edge only within 8 ms of poll activity

   to one, folded in at the next poll, since at most one buffer can be pending. Inside normal
   polling nothing is collapsed, so a chattering pin still shows as an excess. `reportInt()`
   waits one scan for the last buffer's edge and also prints `INT_EDGES_RAW`. The pull-up was
   dropped. The QEMU gate still passes (23 edges / 22 buffers, nothing collapsed).

## 6n. RT1176 core pinMode: pull-up, pull-down and open drain (2026-11-18)

`pinMode()` in `teensy-cores/imxrt1176/digital.c` wrote pad value 0x0008 for every mode and
only changed GDIR, so:

- `INPUT_PULLUP` and `INPUT_PULLDOWN` were plain inputs. On the pads both pin tables use
  (GPIO_AD, GPIO_LPSR, GPIO_DISP_B2), 0x0008 is PUS=1 with PUE=0, pull disabled. The code
  comment called it "DSE"; DSE is bit 1.
- `OUTPUT_OPENDRAIN` was a push-pull output: no ODE bit was set.

Pad fields on those groups: bit 0 SRE, bit 1 DSE, bit 2 PUE (pull enable), bit 3 PUS (1 = up),
ODE at bit 4 on GPIO_AD / GPIO_DISP_B2 but bit 5 (ODE_LPSR) on GPIO_LPSR. GPIO_EMC_B*,
GPIO_DISP_B1 and GPIO_SD_B* pads use a different 2-bit PULL field instead; no Arduino pin is on
them today, and the code comment says so.

| Mode | Pad value now |
|---|---|
| `INPUT`, `OUTPUT` | 0x0008 (unchanged, no pull) |
| `INPUT_PULLUP` | 0x000C (PUE + PUS) |
| `INPUT_PULLDOWN` | 0x0004 (PUE) |
| `OUTPUT_OPENDRAIN` | 0x0018 on GPIO_AD / DISP_B2, 0x0028 on GPIO_LPSR (chosen by whether the pad register is in the IOMUXC_LPSR block at 0x40C08000); no pull, as on Teensy 4 |

Silicon checks on Nosh, six spare GPIO_AD pins (Arduino 0, 1, 2, 7, 16, 17), console read over
SWD:

- `pull_test`: PULLUP / PULLDOWN / PULLUP read 1 / 0 / 1 on every pin, pad 0x0C / 0x04 / 0x0C.
- `od_test` (pull added by hand after `pinMode`, SION set so PSR reads the pad):

  | Mode | Write | Pad | Read |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | push-pull, pull-down | 1 | 0x04 | 1 (driven) |
  | open drain, pull-down | 1 | 0x14 | 0 (released, pull wins) |
  | open drain, pull-down | 0 | 0x14 | 0 |
  | open drain, pull-up | 1 / 0 / 1 | 0x1C | 1 / 0 / 1 |

  Pin 14 (GPIO_LPSR_04, I2C5 SDA) in open drain reads pad 0x28: ODE_LPSR, not bit 4. Only the
  pad was checked there, with the line released high so the bus was left alone.

Both builds: `PULL_TEST OK fails=0`, `OD_TEST OK fails=0`. The blink QEMU gates pass on rt1176
and nosh.

| evkb | `44b9bfc`, `7b4d9b5` | `evkb.cmake` cores pin bumped to each; forced-fetch blink builds resolve the pin and compile the same `digital.c` as the local checkout |

## 6r. Host-side reset without SW1 (2026-11-18)

Every flash so far needed a press of SW1. Two routes were tried on the second board.

**Probe nRESET: does not work yet.** J1 pin 10 reaches POR_B through D1, the node SW1 shorts to
ground, so the probe's nRESET should do what SW1 does. With the Atmel-ICE asked to assert
nRESET (CMSIS-DAP SWJ_Pins), the probe still read its own pin 10 as high, and the board did not
reset (J11 kept printing `alive=`, SRSR stayed clear). The line is not going low at the probe,
so the cable (pin 10 not carried) or the Atmel-ICE firmware is the suspect; D1 cannot cause a
high reading on the probe side. Not yet measured: J1.10 and TP7 (POR_B) with a meter while
nRESET is held.

**Warm reset from the host: the ROM parks.** With pyOCD's RT1176 hooks disabled (no trap code,
SRMR left alone), the state after a SW1 boot is clean: SRMR = 0x0CC0 (M7REQ_RESET_MODE = 00,
reset system) and GPR26 = 0x4000 (boot VTOR = the ROM at 0x00200000). Writing AIRCR =
0x05FA0004 over SWD, without halting, resets the whole SoC: SRSR reads `M7_REQUEST`. The ROM
then reads the FCB (FlexSPI1 LUT0 = 0x0A2004EC, the image's 4-byte 0xEC quad read), the IVT is
readable at 0x30001000, but the M7 ends up at 0x00223104 with VTOR = 0x00210000 and never jumps
to the image. Nothing shows on USB, so it is not the serial downloader. Ruled out:

- The debugger: clearing DHCSR.C_DEBUGEN and DEMCR vector catch before the reset, and exiting
  without closing the pyOCD session, gives the same park.
- Persistent registers: the SRC GPRs are all zero.

The routine at 0x2230EC waits for IOMUXC_LPSR_GPR41 bit 24, reads OCOTP at 0x40CAC0B0, then
executes `b .` at 0x223104. Just above it, 0x2230C8 is the ROM's own SYSRESETREQ routine (its
loop at 0x2230E8 is the address seen in section 6d), and every exception vector in the ROM's
table at 0x210000 points to it. Nothing in the readable ROM calls the park routine: its caller is
in 0x200000-0x20FFFF, which faults when read over SWD. Why a warm boot parks is still unknown.

**Working reset: start the image from the ROM park.** When the ROM parks it has already done
the slow part: the SoC is reset and FlexSPI is configured from the image's own FCB. The host
finishes the job:

1. Attach without halting (pyOCD with the no-trap user script); refuse if SRMR masks M7
   requests.
2. Write AIRCR = 0x05FA0004 (skipped if the core is already parked, as it is right after a
   pyOCD flash).
3. Poll the DWT PC sample (0xE000101C, readable without a halt) until it reads 0x00223104.
4. Halt in the ROM loop, where no application code and no I2C transfer is running, so the
   LPI2C debug-halt wedge (section 6m) cannot happen.
5. Load MSP = `_estack` from the ELF, PC = the IVT entry at 0x30001004, xPSR = 0x01000000,
   VTOR = 0x30001000; resume; clear DHCSR.C_DEBUGEN.

Results on the second board, every one without SW1:

| Run | Image | J11 |
|---|---|---|
| reset of a running image, twice | `nosh_bringup` | `reset: srsr=0x08000002`, `BRINGUP=PASS` both times |
| flash then start | `pull_test` | `PULL_TEST OK fails=0` |
| flash then start (flash script) | `nosh_bringup` | `reset: srsr=0x18000006 M7_LOCKUP`, `BRINGUP=PASS` |

The flash script now runs the start step after every flash. Caveat: the image is started by the
host rather than by the ROM, so whatever the ROM does between the park and its normal jump is
skipped. Nothing tested so far behaves differently from a SW1 boot: SDRAM, SD, I2C, GPIO.

**About SRC_SRSR.** The flags are sticky until written with 1s or the board is power-cycled;
SW1 (POR_B) sets `POR` but does not clear the others. So the second board's
`0x18010007 POR M7_LOCKUP` in section 6q was history, not that run. The `M7_LOCKUP` in the last
row above is the same: SRSR had not been cleared since several earlier runs. Cleared immediately
before a flash-and-start, it reads `0x08000002` (M7_REQUEST only), so a flash on its own does not
set the lockup flag; which earlier step did is not known. Clearing SRSR before a run makes the
reading mean that run only.

## 6s. Second board: audio loopback recording, line out J8 to line in J9 (2026-11-18)

A stereo cable joins line out (J8) to line in (J9). This is the first test of the SGTL5000's

**Test.** A scratch sketch (`loopback_test`, Nosh build, local Audio checkout) plays a 1000 Hz

shorted channel would show. `AudioInputI2S` feeds one `AudioRecordQueue` per channel. Codec:
`enable()`, `inputSelect(AUDIO_INPUT_LINEIN)`, `lineInLevel(0)`, `lineOutLevel(13)`. After 500 ms

**First run: nothing recorded.** `codec enable=1`, but both queues stayed empty for 5 s.
`AudioMemoryUsage` was 0, so the audio graph had never run. SAI1_TCSR read `0x90170000`: TE and
BCE set but not FRDE, so the TX DMA request was off, the output's DMA never fired and its ISR
never called `update_all()`. Cause, in the Audio library: `AudioInputI2S::begin()` calls

IMXRT1062 branch has a guard that returns early if TX or RX is already enabled; the RT1176
branch had none. Fixed by adding the same guard (Audio 0db101d). Output-only and input-only
sketches are unchanged, since TE and RE are both clear on a fresh boot; every sketch that uses
both I2S nodes on the RT1176 needed this, EVKB included.

**With the fix:** TCSR = RCSR = `0x90100001` (TE, BCE, FRDE), and each channel fills 344 blocks
in 997 ms, so the recording keeps real time.

## 6t. Second board: RTC and the 32.768 kHz crystal Y2 (2026-11-18)

Y2 is an Epson FC-135 (Q13FC13500002, CL 7 pF, ±20 ppm) on RTC_XTALI/RTC_XTALO (T13/U13)
with 10 pF load caps C143/C144. The SNVS domain has no backup battery: VDD_SNVS_IN comes from
the 3.3 V LDO (U7) through D5, so the RTC keeps time across a warm reset but not across a
power cycle. The core already runs the SNVS RTC (startup.c starts the LP secure counter with a
2019 seed if it is stopped, then syncs the HP counter from it; `rtc_get()` reads HP).

**Test.** A scratch sketch (`rtc_xtal_test`, Nosh build) reads the oscillator status, checks
`rtc_get()` against `micros()`, and times the HP RTC's 32768 Hz counter against the CM7 cycle
counter over three 10 s windows. The CPU clock is exactly 24 MHz × 83 / 2 = 996 MHz, so this
measures Y2 against Y1. The first boot sets a known time; a host reset (section 6r) 60 s later
checks that it carried over.

```
snvs: GPR33=0x00180000 xtal_ok=1 GPR34=0x00000000 LPCR=0x00000021 HPCR=0x00000001 ...
XTAL_OK=PASS
tick 1: rtc_get=1800000002 +1000120 us
tick 2: rtc_get=1800000003 +2000241 us
tick 3: rtc_get=1800000004 +3000362 us
RTC_TICK=PASS
window 0: ticks=327680 cycles=9961200311 err=-120.50 ppm
window 1: ticks=327680 cycles=9961200257 err=-120.49 ppm
window 2: ticks=327680 cycles=9961200236 err=-120.49 ppm
freq: mean=-120.49 ppm  f=32764.052 Hz
(host reset 60 s later)
phase2: rtc_get=KNOWN+60 s
RTC_PERSIST=PASS
freq: mean=-120.43 ppm  f=32764.054 Hz
```

- The crystal oscillates: IOMUXC_SNVS_GPR GPR33 bit 20 (SNVS_XTAL_CLK_OK) is set at boot, and
  the frequency is stable to 0.01 ppm between windows, which rules out the internal RC
  oscillator.
- `rtc_get()` steps once a second, and the time survives a warm reset.

**Which crystal is off.** The board can only compare Y2 with Y1. To separate them, the HP RTC
counter (0x40C90024/28) and DWT CYCCNT were read over SWD every 0.5 s for 600 s, without
halting, and fitted against the Mac's clock. `sntp` against time.apple.com before and after
showed the Mac drifting about 3 ppm in that time.

| Crystal | Against the host | Per day |
|---|---|---|
| Y2, 32.768 kHz (RTC) | **−102.4 ppm** | 8.8 s slow |
| Y1, 24 MHz (CPU) | +19.4 ppm | 1.7 s fast |
| Y2 against Y1 | −121.8 ppm (the board's own reading) | |

So most of the error is Y2. Y1 running fast agrees with the design review's note that its
4.7 pF load caps (C53/C54) look small.

**The RT1176 adds load capacitance of its own.** The design-time assumption was that the RTC
pins have only pin parasitics (1.5-2 pF), so 10 pF caps plus about 3 pF of routing would land
near the crystal's 7 pF. That is wrong. GPR34 (0x40C98088) has a 4-bit trim for an internal
load capacitor on the 32 kHz oscillator, SNVS_OSC_CAP_TRIM in bits 27:24. It is used when
SNVS_CAP_TRIM_SEL (bit 23) is 1; otherwise the trim comes from eFuse. A second scratch sketch
(`rtc_trim_sweep`) stepped through every code. Y2 against Y1, 5 s per code:

| Code | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 0 (= fuse) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ppm | −74.2 | −82.0 | −89.1 | −95.4 | −101.3 | −106.6 | −111.4 | −115.9 | −120.0 | −123.8 | −127.3 | −130.6 | −133.7 | −136.4 | −139.0 | −141.5 |

The code is two's complement: 8 is the least load, 7 the most, and this chip's fuse value
equals code 0. The oscillator stayed up at every code. Even the lightest setting leaves the
crystal over-loaded, so the internal capacitance plus 10 pF on C143/C144 is more than this
7 pF crystal wants.

**Ways to fix it** (none applied yet):

1. Trim only: code 8 at startup gives about −55 ppm against true time (−74.2 + 19.4),
   4.7 s/day. Measured.
2. Trim and calibration: SNVS_LPCR and SNVS_HPCR have a calibration field (LPCALB / HPCALB,
   ±1 count per 32768 cycles, 30.5 ppm per count). Code 9 plus 2 counts works out to about
   −2 ppm. Calculated, not tested. The core's `rtc_compensate()` is an empty stub.
3. Hardware: about 5 pF on C143/C144 instead of 10 pF should come close to 0 ppm at the fuse
   trim. This is an estimate from the trim's ppm-per-step and the crystal's pulling; try it on
   one board first.

The RTC and Y2 work on the second board; the only problem is the frequency. Both sketches are
scratch builds, not evkb examples.

## 6u. Second board: SW2 (ONOFF) and SW3 (WAKEUP) (2026-11-18)

Wiring, from the netlist: SW3 pulls the WAKEUP pad (T8, GPIO13_IO00 at ALT5) to GND, with C57
100 nF; SW2 pulls ONOFF (U10) to GND, with C56 100 nF. Both pads have a weak pull-up at reset
(pad control 0x0E). ONOFF goes to the SNVS power-button logic (its SRC_RESET_B function is
used only in test mode). PMIC_ON_REQ (U9) drives BUCK_EN, the enable of the main 3.3 V buck
U6, through R28 10 k. The SNVS domain runs from the separate LDO U7 through D5, so it stays
powered when the buck is off.

**Test.** A scratch sketch (`button_test`, Nosh build) muxes WAKEUP to GPIO13_IO00, polls it
and the SNVS button state every 1 ms (SNVS_HPSR.BTN, SNVS_LPSR.SPO) and logs each press and
release with its duration on J11. At boot it prints SRC_SRSR and SNVS_LPSR, then clears them.
J11 was recorded by a capture that reopens the port if it disappears.

**Short presses.** Three of each:

```
  572755 SW2 ONOFF BTN=1 (#1)
  572805 SW2 LPSR.SPO set (#1) LPSR=0x40040008 -> W1C
  573249 SW2 ONOFF BTN=0  held 494 ms
  ... #2 held 343 ms, #3 held 383 ms
  590593 SW3 WAKEUP down  (#1)
  592381 SW3 WAKEUP up    held 1788 ms
  ... #2 held 2056 ms, #3 held 1941 ms
```

- SW3: GPIO13_IO00 low while pressed, high on release. Each press counted exactly once at
  1 ms sampling, so there is no contact bounce to speak of.
- SW2: SNVS_HPSR.BTN is 1 while held, and SNVS_LPSR.SPO (set power off) sets 50 ms into each
  press, matching the default 50 ms debounce (SNVS_LPCR.DEBOUNCE = 00).
- SNVS_HPSR.BI (button interrupt) never set: the button interrupt is not configured
  (SNVS_HPCR = 0x00000001, BTN_CONFIG/BTN_MASK at reset). A configuration matter, not hardware.

**Long press: power off and back on.** SW2 held for about 7 s, then pressed again briefly:

```
 2135660 SW2 ONOFF BTN=1 (#4)
 2135710 SW2 LPSR.SPO set (#4) LPSR=0x40040008 -> W1C
 2140000 beat: ... BTN=1                    <- last line; J11 then silent
 === BOOT ===                               <- after the second press
boot: SRSR=0x00010001 LPSR=0x40020008 (EO=1 SPO=0) HPSR=0x80009b40 (BTN=1 BI=0)

      33 SW2 ONOFF BTN=1 (#1)
```

- The long press switched the board off: the last line is 4.3 s into the press and the next

- The second press switched it back on. SRC_SRSR = 0x00010001 is a power-on reset on both

- The RTC kept counting while the board was off. It was set to a known time in section 6t; read

This proves both buttons, the SNVS power-button path through PMIC_ON_REQ, R28 and BUCK_EN, and
a clean restart of the 3.3 V buck. Not tested: the SNVS button interrupt (not enabled) and SW3

## 6w. Second board: RK055 scanout under SDRAM load (2026-11-18)

Section 6l showed no LCDIFv2 underruns with nothing else using SDRAM, but left two things open:
the `FB_SUM` check, and scanout while other bus masters also use SDRAM. Nosh runs the SDRAM
(SEMC) at 132 MHz against the EVKB's 198 MHz, so this was the main display risk.

**Test.** A scratch sketch (`sdram_load_test`, Nosh build, MipiDisplay `panels/rk055`) puts
the panel up and draws its own bars-and-diagonal pattern into the SDRAM framebuffer. It then
runs one phase per load, 600 frames each (about 10 s at 58.7 Hz). At every VSYNC it reads and
clears the LCDIFv2 UNDERRUN latch (INT_STATUS_D0 bit 1), so the count is frames with an
underrun out of frames scanned. All loads copy SDRAM to SDRAM, and each copy is compared with
its source afterwards:

- **CPU:** 8x-unrolled word copies of 2 MB, in 4 KB slices. The D-cache is off in this core, so
  every access reaches the SEMC. Newlib-nano's `memcpy` is byte-wise and much slower.
- **PXP:** back-to-back 720x1280 RGB565 copies (1.84 MB each).
- **eDMA:** 2 MB copies in 32-byte bursts (one manual-start TCD).

Copy rates below are MB/s copied; each byte copied is one SEMC read plus one write. The sketch
was built at both pixel depths the examples use.

| Phase | 16 bpp: underruns | 16 bpp: copy MB/s CPU / PXP / eDMA | 32 bpp: underruns | 32 bpp: copy MB/s CPU / PXP / eDMA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| idle | 0/600 | - | 0/600 | - |
| CPU | 0/657 | 8 / - / - | 0/600 | 8 / - / - |
| PXP | 0/600 | - / 84 / - | 0/601 | - / 61 / - |
| eDMA | 0/601 | - / - / 59 | 0/600 | - / - / 48 |
| all three | 0/600 | 5 / 45 / 11 | 0/601 | 4 / 39 / 9 |

- Scanout needs about 110 MB/s at 16 bpp (RGB565, the panel test's format) and 221 MB/s at
  32 bpp (XRGB8888, what the LVGL examples use). The heaviest case, 32 bpp with the PXP copying,
  puts about 340 MB/s on the SEMC with no underruns.
- Every copied buffer matched its source, and the framebuffer checksum still matched after all
  phases: `FB_SUM=0x9831F3C5` (16 bpp) and `FB_SUM=0xDA343EC5` (32 bpp), both PASS. This closes
  the `FB_SUM` item from section 6l.
- For reference, `lvgl_pxp_copy_bench` on the EVKB timed a full-frame RGB565 PXP copy under a
  32 bpp scanout at about 114 MB/s, against 61 MB/s here. Nosh's SEMC clock is two-thirds of the
  EVKB's, and this figure includes the polling between copies, so it is not a like-for-like
  comparison.

**Positive control.** No transcript, EVKB or Nosh, had ever shown a non-zero underrun count, so
a zero needed proof that the detector can fire. The layer was pointed at QSPI flash (XIP at
0x30000000, far slower than the scanout needs) for 30 frames: 30/30 underran, and INT_STATUS_D0
bit 24 also set. The detector works.

**Recovery after an underrun depends on depth.** The layer was then pointed back at the SDRAM
framebuffer and monitored once a second:

- 16 bpp: only the switch-over frame underran; clean from the next frame on.
- 32 bpp: every frame kept underrunning for about 30 s, then it stopped by itself and stayed
  clean for the remaining 103 s of monitoring. This repeated on three runs.

The likely reading is that the LCDIFv2 has to catch up from its own lag, and at 32 bpp it has
little spare bandwidth to do it, but that is not proven. What the panel shows during those 30 s
was not observed. It is controller behaviour, not specific to the Nosh board, and was not checked
on the EVKB. It does not affect the load result, but at 32 bpp a single real underrun could leave
the display disturbed for about 30 s.

## 6x. Second board: RTC firmware trim (2026-11-19)

Fix 2 from section 6t, tested: the 32 kHz load-cap trim in GPR34 plus the SNVS calibration.
LPCALB_VAL (SNVS_LPCR, for the LP counter) and HPCALB_VAL (SNVS_HPCR, for the HP counter that
`rtc_get()` reads) are 5-bit two's-complement fields in bits 14:10. Each count adds or drops one
count per 32768 ticks, about 30.5 ppm, and each field's enable is bit 8.

**Test.** A scratch sketch (`rtc_cal_test`, Nosh build) applies a setting, waits 3 s for the
oscillator to settle, then measures the HP and LP counters against the CM7 cycle counter
(Y1 × 83/2) over 60 s windows. With the final setting held, `hostref.py` (section 6t) sampled the
HP counter and CYCCNT over SWD for 600 s against the Mac's clock.

| Setting | Y2 vs Y1, board, 60 s windows (HP and LP) | Y2 vs Mac, SWD, 600 s |
|---|---|---|
| fuse trim, no calibration | −120.5 ppm | −102.4 ppm (section 6t) |
| trim code 9 (GPR34 = 0x09800000), +2 counts | −20.85 ppm, all 14 windows | **−4.8 ppm** |
| trim code 14 (GPR34 = 0x0E800000), +3 counts | −19.84 ppm | about −3.8 ppm (calculated) |

```
apply trim=9 cal=+2 -> GPR34=0x09800000 LPCR=0x00000921 (cal +2 en 1) HPCR=0x00000901 (cal +2 en 1)
hold  1   60s: hp=-20.85 lp=-20.85 xtal_ok=1
  ... 12 hold windows, all identical
samples=1182 span=599.9 s
Y2 (RTC 32.768 kHz) vs host: -4.77 ppm   (residual 0.12 ms rms)
Y1 (CPU 24 MHz x 83/2) vs host: +16.30 ppm
Y2 vs Y1 (what the board measures): -21.07 ppm
```

- Code 9 with +2 counts takes Y2 from −102.4 ppm (8.8 s/day slow) to about −4.8 ppm
  (0.4 s/day). Section 6t predicted −1.6 ppm. Y1 read +16.3 ppm against the Mac this time
  (+19.4 in 6t), and the Mac itself drifted about 3 ppm in 6t, so the host reference is good to
  a few ppm.
- The HP and LP counters gave identical figures in every window, so both calibrations work and
  agree. The oscillator stayed up (XTAL_OK) at every setting.
- One calibration count is 30.5 ppm, so the trim code is the only finer adjustment. Code 9/+2
  and code 14/+3 are about 1 ppm apart. Code 8/+2 would be about +2 ppm, not clearly better
  given the host's accuracy.

**Two rules for the firmware, both found in this test:**

1. **HPCALB_VAL is ignored while HPCALB_EN is still set, and clearing EN is not immediate.**
   The first run cleared EN and wrote the new value straight away: LPCR moved from +2 to +3,
   but HPCR stayed at +2 (HP then read −50.35 ppm while LP read −19.84). Polling until EN reads
   back 0 fixes it. That took 240 polls, about one 32 kHz cycle; LPCR's EN clears at once. With
   the poll, both moved to +3 and read −19.84 ppm.
2. **HPCR is reset by a warm reset, GPR34 and LPCR are not.** After a host reset (section 6r)
   the sketch's first line read GPR34 = 0x09800000 and LPCR = 0x00000921, still set, but
   HPCR = 0x00000001, calibration off. With no backup battery, a cold power-up clears all
   three. So startup should write the trim and both calibrations on every boot.

The core is unchanged: `rtc_compensate()` is still an empty stub and startup applies no trim.

## 6y. Second board: DJ deck decode speed and the core's memcpy (2026-11-20)

`audio/dj_deck` (evkb) loads a whole track into SDRAM before it plays: decode,
then a spectrum and tempo pass for the waveform and beat grid. Loading was slow, so the decoders
were profiled on the board and the four largest costs were rewritten. The biggest single cost
turned out to be in the core, not the codecs.

**Method.** `audio_codecs_probe` built with `-DPROBE_PROFILE=1` turns on stage timers in the
AudioCodecs library and prints a `PROF` line per stage. All figures are for 8 s clips on Nosh.
Each change went in test-first on the host (the library's 90 tests pass), then was measured on
the board; the probe still ends `CODECS_PROBE=PASS`.

| Change | Stage, before -> after | Whole pass, before -> after |
|---|---|---|
| MP3 synthesis filter: fast 32-point DCT and a ring buffer instead of a 64x32 matrix | - | MP3 decode 1056 -> 537 ms |
| MP3 IMDCT: 9-point DCTs, silent subbands skipped | - | MP3 decode 537 -> 400 ms |
| Bit reader reads a word at a time; FLAC Rice decoder works from a 32-bit window | Rice 407 -> 69 ms | FLAC decode 482 -> 142 ms; MP3 400 -> 373 ms |
| Spectrum FFT: 1024-point real transform as a 512-point complex one | FFT 132 -> 68 ms | spectrum 437 -> 373 ms |
| Core `memcpy`: the Teensy 4 assembly routine | PCM read 280 -> 73 ms | spectrum 373 -> 160 ms; tempo 82 -> 43 ms |

- The FLAC output checksum is unchanged (0xA12996D4), and the spectrum and tempo files the probe
  writes are byte-identical to before the FFT change.
- The MP3 decoder is now nearer the double-precision reference than it was: the new synthesis
  filter and IMDCT are within 1e-5 of it, where the old synthesis filter was off by up to 1.2e-4.
- After these five an 8 s MP3 decodes in about 370 ms, with synthesis still the largest stage
  at 166 ms. A second pass on the synthesis filter, below, takes it to 283 ms.

**The core's `memcpy`.** The RT1176 core linked newlib-nano's `memcpy`, a 28-byte loop that
moves one byte at a time. The D-cache is off in this core (section 6w), so with buffers in SDRAM
every byte is its own SEMC access, about 0.2 us each. The spectrum pass copies PCM out of SDRAM
in small blocks, and that copy was 280 of its 437 ms. The core now carries
`imxrt1176/memcpy-armv7m.S`, the same file the Teensy 4 core ships (308 bytes linked). This
changes every RT1176 image that calls `memcpy`, on the EVKB as well as Nosh.

**DJ deck load times**, from the deck's bench build, first load / reload of the same file:

| File | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `short.wav` | 1462 / 1047 ms | 965 / 743 ms |
| `short.aiff` | 1679 / 1049 ms | 1182 / 740 ms |
| `click.flac` | 3722 / 1978 ms | 2059 / 1166 ms |
| `click.mp3` | 4546 / 2953 ms | 2351 / 1424 ms |

**Checks.**

- Every RT1176 and Nosh gate image was rebuilt against the new core and each main ELF checked
  for the 308-byte `memcpy`. The full QEMU sweep then passed: 169 gates (137 EVKB, 25 Nosh,
  7 RT1062). QEMU does not model memory timing, so the sweep shows nothing broke, not the gain.
- On silicon the new `memcpy` has run only on Nosh (the probe and the DJ deck), not on an EVKB.
- `evkb.cmake` pins are bumped (evkb 8a4c512): cores 7c93711, AudioCodecs 9cfbdfa, Audio 4ef7bb6,
  SynthUI 4070a36. All four are on the default branch of their repos. The cores pin also

- A forced-fetch Nosh build of `dj_deck` (`-DEVKB_BOARD=nosh -DEVKB_FORCE_FETCH=ON`, 19
  dependencies fetched from GitHub) resolves those four pins and builds. Its 3072 sized symbols

| Change | Synthesis | MP3 decode |
|---|---|---|

## 6z. DJ deck: AUTO, the next track by itself (2026-11-20)

`audio/dj_deck` has an AUTO switch above the pitch fader (off at power-up). With it on:

- When a track plays out by itself, the deck loads the next audio file in that track's folder,
  in the browser's order, and plays it. It stops at the last file; it does not wrap. A pause, a
  seek or a scratch to the end does not count as the track ending.
- A file that will not load (too long, unsupported, read error) is skipped, with "skipped
  <name>" on the screen. This applies to files the deck loaded by itself; one picked by hand
  that fails stays failed.
- **Early load.** In the last 5 s of a track that is fully loaded and analysed, the next file is
  loaded beside it in SDRAM while the current one plays on and stays fully usable. When the
  track ends the waiting one goes on the deck at once.
- **No room for both.** If the next track's audio cannot be allocated beside the playing one,
  the early load is dropped and the track is loaded after the current one has ended and been
  freed. Tracks are limited to 96 MB of PCM each (about 9 minutes) and SDRAM is 128 MB, so two
  tracks of roughly five minutes or more do not fit together.

A ring buffer, with the next track written over the played part of the current one, was
considered and not used: it would break scratching back, REW, CUE and the waveform in a track's
last seconds, and the player needs each track as one contiguous block.

**How it is built.** `Loader::startKeeping()` starts a load and hands the loaded track's PCM,
peak tables and beats to the caller, so they stay valid until the swap. The decisions (load
early, swap, give up for lack of memory, skip, play) are a pure state machine in `deck_math.h`.

**Checks.**

- Host tests for the state machine and for `startKeeping()` (the kept track untouched, the
  out-of-memory case, no leaked allocations).
- New QEMU gate `dj_deck[auto]`: a card with a 2 s clip, a fake `.wav` and the clip again;
  scripted touches load the first, switch AUTO on and press PLAY.

```
UI_AUTO=1
UI_PLAY=1
AUTO_PRELOAD /music/b_bad.wav
LOAD_FAIL unsupported
AUTO_SKIP /music/b_bad.wav unsupported
AUTO_PRELOAD /music/c_two.wav
LOAD_DONE ms=762
AUTO_NEXT /music/c_two.wav
AUTO_PLAY gap_ms=3
AUTO_END
```

- Both `dj_deck` gates pass. The silence between the two tracks was 3 ms in QEMU.
- On the second board the image is flashed and reaches `DJ_READY`.

**Not checked.** The switch has not been used by touch on hardware, and the gap has not been
measured on the board with real tracks (the console prints `AUTO_PLAY gap_ms=N`). The no-room
fallback is covered by host tests only: the gate's clips always fit. A track loaded early for
the first time may show its beat grid some seconds after it starts.

Commit: evkb f327fd7.
