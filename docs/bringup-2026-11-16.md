# Nosh bring-up, 2026-11-16

First power-up of the assembled Nosh board (JLCPCB order Y95-2611336A, 5 PCB + 2 PCBA,
placed 2026-11-03). One board on the bench, powered from the USB-C device port J3, Atmel-ICE
on the SWD header J1. No console adapter on J11 and no microSD card were available, so the
verdicts below were read out of RAM over SWD rather than from the serial transcript.

## Result in one line

The board boots `nosh_bringup` from its QSPI NOR with SW4 = 10, the user LED blinks at 2 Hz,
the 128 MB SDRAM passes its data, address and fill tests, the microSD mounts and round-trips a
file, and nothing on the board needed a rework. By the end of the day the sketch reports
`BRINGUP=PASS`. The one defect found was in software: a NACK race in the Wire library's
LPI2C driver that made a bus scan report every second address, fixed and verified the same
day (Wire 0d80418).

## 1. The charge-only USB-C cable

Symptom: the board was clearly powered (chip answering over SWD, see below) but the Mac never
enumerated anything from J3, in any boot mode.

Cause: the first USB-C cable carried VBUS but no D+/D-. Swapping to a known-good data cable
made the ROM serial downloader appear immediately as `SE Blank RT Family`, VID 0x1fc9
PID 0x013d.

Lesson: a Nosh that powers up but shows no USB device is first of all a cable question. The
board side of J3 (16-pin USB 2.0 receptacle, Rd 5.1 k on both CC pins, RClamp0854P ESD) is
fine.

## 2. Boot-mode switch SW4

SW4 is the KingTek DSHP02TS 2-way slide DIP. Pad-to-net mapping from the PCB:

| SW4 position | Pads | Series resistor | Strap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 and 4 | R11 4.7 k | BOOT_MODE1 (GPIO_LPSR_03) |
| 2 | 2 and 3 | R12 4.7 k | BOOT_MODE0 (GPIO_LPSR_02) |

Pads 1 and 2 are +3V3; both straps have 100 k pull-downs, so "off" reads 0.

| BOOT_MODE[1:0] | SW4 | ROM behaviour on Nosh |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | both off | "boot from fuses". What was observed on the day: both cores parked in branch-to-self loops (CM7 at 0x00223104, CM4 at 0x0023f040), SRC_SCR with BT_RELEASE_M4 set, nothing on USB. **That was pyOCD, not the ROM** (see section 6d): its RT1176 target parks both cores on exactly those two addresses and releases the CM4 on every connect, and the USB silence was the charge-only cable. **Re-tested 2026-11-17 with the probe off J1 and a data cable in J3: 00 enumerates the serial downloader (`SE Blank RT Family` 1fc9:013d), exactly as the RM says for blank fuses.** |
| 01 | pos 2 on | serial downloader on USB1/J3, enumerates as 1fc9:013d |
| 10 | pos 1 on | internal boot from the W25Q512 on FlexSPI1 |

The boot-mode word is `SRC_SBMR2` at 0x40C0400C, BMOD in bits 25:24. Reading it is the
quick way to confirm what the ROM latched after a reset.

## 3. Flashing over SWD with pyOCD

pyOCD's built-in RT1170 target has a 16 MB FlexSPI flash region at 0x30000000 (4 KB sectors,
256 B pages) and its loader programmed the 3.3 V W25Q512JV without complaint.

Do **not** hand pyOCD the ELF. The core's ITCM code (`.text.itcm`) has VMA 0 and LMA
0x300022d0; a section-by-VMA loader would skip it or try to write ITCM. Build a flash-only
binary by load address first:

```bash
arm-none-eabi-objcopy -O binary \
  -j .text.headers -j .text.code -j .text.itcm -j .fini -j .ARM.exidx -j .data -j .text.csf \
  build-nosh/nosh_bringup.elf nosh_bringup.bin
```

`.vectorsram`, `.bss`, `.bss.dma` and `.bss.extram` are RAM-only and must stay out. Check the
image before programming: the FCB tag `FCFB` at offset 0x400 and the IVT tag 0xD1 at 0x1000
are what the RT1170 ROM looks for.

```bash
pyocd flash -t mimxrt1170_cm7 -a 0x30000000 -e sector nosh_bringup.bin
```

Result for the 55,296-byte image: 14 sectors erased, 216 pages programmed, about 7.8 kB/s.
Read-back with `pyocd cmd -t mimxrt1170_cm7 -M attach -c "savemem 0x30000000 55296 rb.bin"`
compared byte-identical.

The serial downloader path (SW4 = 01, SPSDK `blhost` plus the RT1170 flashloader) is the
documented alternative in the README; it was not exercised because SPSDK is not installed
here and the SWD path worked first time.

## 6. Open items

**microSD on uSDHC2.** Done, see the passing run above.

**I2C5 scan: every odd address acknowledges. FIXED the same day, verified on the board:
`I2C5_SCAN=DONE n=0` with the rebuilt image (build Nov 16 2026 13:41:23).** It was a
driver defect, not the bus. In
`Wire/lpi2c1176.c`, `lpi2c1176_master_write()` judges ACK/NACK by waiting for the STOP
(SDF) with NDF as the error mask. When the LPI2C master sees a NACK it sets NDF and
generates a STOP on its own; `lpi2c1176_wait_flag()` clears NDF and returns the moment it
sees it, and `lpi2c1176_bus_recover()` resets the FIFOs, both before that hardware STOP has
completed. The next transaction (the odd address) queues START and STOP, waits for SDF, and
is satisfied by the previous transaction's STOP completing, so it returns 0 = ACK. Its own
NACK is then seen correctly by the transaction after it, and the pattern alternates: n = 56
of 112, all odd. The fix (Wire commit 0d80418, pinned in `evkb.cmake`): on NDF,
`wait_flag` waits for SDF before clearing anything; `bus_recover` waits for MBF to clear before
resetting the FIFOs; `master_read` no longer queues a second STOP after an address NACK. The
QEMU gate still passes (its scan finds the three modelled devices) and the board reports
n = 0, which is the right answer for a bus with only an unclocked SGTL5000 on it.

**Two reflash lessons from the second image.**

- *Readback after a flash is skewed.* The pyOCD flash algorithm leaves FlexSPI in its own
  configuration, and AHB reads from 0x30000000 then come back shifted by one byte from the
  FCB onwards (every sampled 256-byte block matched the image at shift +1). The content was
  fine. A readback compare is only meaningful while an image booted by the ROM is running,
  as it was after the first flash. pyOCD's own "pages skipped" count is unreliable for the
  same reason.
- *A freshly flashed image does not boot until SW1.* After pyOCD's post-flash reset the ROM
  wrote AIRCR SYSRESETREQ (0x05FA0004) and spun at 0x002230e8 waiting for a reset that never
  came, because SRC_SRMR held M7REQ_RESET_MODE = "do not reset". The first reading of this
  blamed the ROM and a skewed header read; the real actor is pyOCD, see section 6d. Only SW1
  or a power cycle boots a freshly flashed image; budget for a button press after every flash.

## 6d. What pyOCD's RT1176 target does on every connect

Found while trying to make host-side resets work. pyOCD's built-in `mimxrt1170_cm7` target
(`target_MIMXRT1176xxxxx.py`, `find_aps`) performs three writes each time it connects:

1. `prepare_cm7_trap_code`: a two-word vector table at 0x2001FF00 whose reset entry is
   0x223105, and `IOMUXC_LPSR_GPR26` (the CM7 boot VTOR) pointed at it. 0x223104 is a
   `b .` inside the ROM, which is why the CM7 "parked" there.
2. `release_cm4` + `prepare_cm4_trap_code`: SRC_SCR.BT_RELEASE_M4 = 1 with a vector table at
   0x20250000 whose reset entry is 0x23F041, another ROM `b .`. That is the "CM4 running at
   0x0023f040" and the BT_RELEASE_M4 bit read earlier.
3. `update_sbmr`: ORs 0xF << 10 into SRC_SRMR (it calls it SBMR), i.e. M4REQ and M7REQ reset
   mode = "do not reset anything". That is the 0x3c00 and why every SYSRESETREQ, from pyOCD,
   from the ROM or from firmware, was swallowed while a probe session had touched the chip.

Consequences and corrections:

- The "SW4 = 00 parks both cores" observation in section 2 was this trap code, taken through a
  pyOCD session. It says nothing about the ROM's boot-from-fuses behaviour.
- A power-on reset from SW1 clears GPR26 and SRMR, which is why the button always worked.
- Clearing the mask from firmware was tried and reverted: pyOCD re-arms it on the next attach,
  and with the mask cleared and GPR26 zeroed in the same session, a SYSRESETREQ did reset the
  SoC (SRSR records M7_REQUEST) but the ROM came up in its own park loop at 0x223104 with the
  FCB and IVT perfectly readable through FlexSPI. Why the RT1170 ROM refuses to boot the image
  after a warm M7-requested reset is unresolved; a POR boots it every time.
- A user script that no-ops the three hooks (`-O user_script=pyocd_notrap.py`, in the session
  scratch) keeps SRMR clean across attaches but does not change the ROM's warm-reset
  behaviour, so it does not buy a host-side reboot either.
- Net: on this bench, flash with pyOCD, then press SW1. Reads over SWD remain safe and useful;
  just remember that any attach rewrites GPR26 and SRMR behind your back.

**SGTL5000 did not answer at 0x0A.** Expected here. The sketch never starts SAI1, so the
codec has no SYS_MCLK, and the SGTL5000's control port needs MCLK running; the Teensy Audio
library always has I2S up before `enable()`. The audio examples (`tone_test`,
`i2s_audio_test`) are the real test of the codec and of the LPI2C5 wiring to it.

## 6a. tone_test: the PIT clock was 20x off, on the EVKB too

`audio/tone_test` is not an audio test: it drives a square wave on Arduino pin 9 (GPIO_AD_01,
the unpopulated touch panel's reset line on Nosh) with `tone()` and reads the pad back
through the GPIO data register. First run on the board:

```
e1=3975 e2=7999 e3=0 during=200 after=0
TONE=FAIL
```

1 kHz requested, 20 kHz measured; 2 kHz requested, 40 kHz measured; a 100 ms timed tone that
stopped after 5 ms. Everything was a factor 20 fast. `tone()` sits on `IntervalTimer`, which
sits on PIT1, which is clocked by the BUS clock root. Live registers: `CCM_CLOCK_ROOT2` =
0x401 (mux 4 = SYS_PLL3 480 MHz, divide by 2 = 240 MHz, exactly what `startup.c` programs)
and PIT1 `LDVAL0` = 5999 for a 500 µs half period, i.e. the firmware believed the PIT ran at
12 MHz. The decoder in `IntervalTimer.cpp` (`pit_clock_hz`) only knew mux 0 and treated every
other source as 24 MHz. QEMU's `imxrt_ccm.c` had the identical gap, so the model ran PIT1 at
12 MHz too and the QEMU gate agreed with the wrong firmware. The hardware was the first thing
to disagree.

This is an EVKB bug as much as a Nosh one: every `IntervalTimer` period and every `tone()`
on the RT1176 core has been 20x short on silicon. The QEMU gates could never have caught it
because the model mirrored the firmware's assumption.

Fix (uncommitted at the time of writing): `pit_clock_hz` decodes all eight BUS sources per the
RM (fsl_clock.h `kCLOCK_BUS_ClockRoot_Mux*`, PFD3 computed from the ANADIG FRAC field) and
`imxrt_ccm.c` gets the same table so the model matches silicon. Rebuilt and reflashed:

```
e1=198 e2=399 e3=0 during=99 after=0
TONE=PASS
```

PIT1 `LDVAL0` now reads 0x1D4BF = 119,999 ticks for 500 µs, which is 240 MHz. The counts are
the same numbers the QEMU gate has always printed, now for the right reason.

## 6h. Bluetooth HCI on the M.2 card over LPUART2 (2026-11-17)

`examples/networking/m2_hci_probe` built with `-DEVKB_BOARD=nosh`, both IW416 blobs
(`-DM2RADIO_IW416_FW=.../sdIW416_wlan.bin.inc -DM2RADIO_IW416_BT_FW=.../uartIW416_bt.bin.inc`,
445 KB image), flashed over SWD, SW1.

First run, default options: `bt_fw_download=ok` (142 chunks, 131840 bytes, 0 CRC errors, the
card's own chunk requests received back, so LPUART2 is bidirectional through U11/U12), Wi-Fi
side up as before, then `hci_reset=timeout reason=no_response` at 3 M / 921600 / 460800 /
115200 baud with framing=0 throughout. Live registers over SWD: LPUART2 MODIR = 0 (RXRTSE off),
GPIO_DISP_B2_13 muxed ALT3 = LPUART2_RTS_B. With hardware RTS off that pad idles HIGH, and on
Nosh it reaches the card's CTS input through U11. The IW416 bootloader ignores CTS; the booted
firmware does not transmit while CTS is high. The probe's own `M2_BT_ASSERT_CTS` option
documents exactly this.

Second run, `-DM2_BT_ASSERT_CTS=ON`:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| HCI Reset | `hci_reset=ok attempts=1 timeouts=0 framing=0` |
| Read_Local_Version | hci_ver 15, hci_rev 0x8300, lmp_ver 15, manufacturer 0x0048, lmp_subver 0x109B |
| Read_BD_ADDR | A0:CD:F3:C5:B9:F3 (Wi-Fi MAC + 1) |
| Read_Buffer_Size | ACL 1021 x 7, SCO 120 x 6 |
| Inquiry (GIAC 10.24 s) | complete, 0 devices (nothing discoverable nearby) |
| Heartbeat | `hci=ok`, all counters 0 |

Nosh note: on the EVKB this pad also resets the 1 Gb Ethernet PHY, which is why the assert was
an opt-in GPIO hack there. On Nosh BT_UART_RTS is dedicated and level-shifted to the module, so
the proper setting is the core's hardware receiver flow control (`Serial2.attachRts()`, the
`M2_BT_RTS_FLOW` build option of bt_tone_test); the static assert is the proven fallback. Any
Nosh BT build must enable one of the two or HCI stays silent.

|---|---|---|

| dropped blocks | 166 | **0** |

| L2CAP send busy-retries (`l2drop`) | 518551 | **0** |

the library's reconnect machinery, not a board issue.

## 6k. m2_lwip_test: lwIP DHCP + TCP echo over Wi-Fi (2026-11-17)

`examples/networking/m2_lwip_test` (lwIP's own DHCP client, raw-API TCP echo client sending

| Run | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 1 | No association: `connectStation()` matches the SSID with an exact, case-sensitive `strcmp`, and the configured name differed from the beacon in one letter's case (`m2_sdio_probe` matches case-insensitively, so it had passed). Fixed in the configuration. |

TCP data path all work on silicon.

**INT edge excess: explained and fixed (2026-11-18).** Every hardware run, on both boards,
counted a few more rising INT edges than buffers read (7 to 29), so `TOUCH_OK` was never printed.

1. *Not a floating line.* The RT1176 core's `pinMode(INPUT_PULLUP)` does not enable a pull:
   it writes pad value 0x0008 for every mode, which on a GPIO_AD pad is PUS=1 with PUE=0
   (pull disabled). Writing 0x0C (PUE + PUS, weak pull-up) to `SW_PAD_CTL_PAD_GPIO_AD_00`
   directly, read back as `INT_PAD=0x0000000C`, left the excess unchanged (241 / 230). The
   GT911 drives the line.
2. *Timestamped edges.* A scratch build logged every edge (DWT cycle count) and every poll
   result, poll gap and phase boundary, read back over SWD. The GT911 pulses INT once per scan,
   every 10.2 ms (9.4 to 11.3 ms). 208 of 209 buffers paired one-to-one with an edge; the 209th
   was the last read, whose edge came after the count. All 10 unpaired edges fell in the six
   29-39 ms gaps where the test stops polling to repaint (after each of the five targets, once
   during the swipe):

   | Gap | Edges in gap | Unpaired |
   |---|---|---|
   | 29 ms, after target 1 | 2 | 1 |
   | 35 ms, after target 2 | 4 | 2 |
   | 39 ms, after target 3 | 3 | 2 |
   | 32 ms, after target 4 | 3 | 2 |
   | 29 ms, after target 5 | 3 | 1 |
   | 35 ms, during the swipe | 3 | 2 |

   The GT911 keeps pulsing every scan while its single buffer is unacknowledged, so a pause of
   about three scans gives three edges and one buffer. The QEMU model pulses once per
   acknowledged buffer, which is why the gate's `BUFFERS <= INT_EDGES <= BUFFERS + POLL_FAILS + 1`
   holds in QEMU and never on glass.
3. *Fix in the test, evkb `9529295`.* The ISR counts an edge only within 8 ms of poll activity
   (below the 9.4 ms minimum scan spacing). Edges later than that are in a pause and collapse
   to one, folded in at the next poll, since at most one buffer can be pending. Inside normal
   polling nothing is collapsed, so a chattering pin still shows as an excess. `reportInt()`
   waits one scan for the last buffer's edge and also prints `INT_EDGES_RAW`. The pull-up was
   dropped. The QEMU gate still passes (23 edges / 22 buffers, nothing collapsed).

Nosh run with the fix:

```
TARGETS_OK   SWIPE_OK dir=+x span=60% samples=36   MULTI_OK ids=0,1 sep=900
INT_EDGES_RAW=187  INT_EDGES=172  BUFFERS=172  POLL_FAILS=0  TARGET_RELEASES=5
TOUCH_OK
```

That is the first `TOUCH_OK` on hardware on either board. Touch on Nosh is fully passing.

Side finding, not fixed: `pinMode(INPUT_PULLUP)` / `INPUT_PULLDOWN` are silently ignored by the
RT1176 core (`teensy-cores/imxrt1176/digital.c`), and its `PAD_CTL_DEFAULT` comment calls 0x0008
"DSE", which on GPIO_AD pads is PUS (DSE is bit 1).
